# Community Session Kit

A practical kit for running paid community sessions with disabled people and turning what they say into specific, buildable tasks for the [AI for Accessibility Toolkit](https://github.com/AI-for-Accessibility-Collective/AI-for-Accessibility-Toolkit).

The toolkit's goal is software that adapts to each person, rather than asking people to adapt to it. This kit applies the same principle to research: the session adapts to the participant, and the participant stays in control of what is shared, recorded and acted on.

## Use the files in this order

| Step | File | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `README.md` (this file) | What are the principles and non-negotiables? |
| 1 | `01-plan-and-recruit.md` | Who do we invite, how do we pay them, and how do we make taking part safe and accessible? |
| 2 | `02-discussion-guide.md` | What do we ask, and how does it map to the toolkit? |
| 3 | `03-synthesis-template.md` | How do we turn what people said into tasks a developer can pick up? |

Each step builds on the last. Skipping step 1 to get to the conversation faster is the most common way sessions go wrong.

## Principles

1. **Needs, not conditions.** The toolkit's Ability profile records what a person asks for, such as larger text or voice control, not a diagnosis. Sessions follow the same rule: we never ask anyone to disclose a condition, and findings are written up in terms of needs.
2. **People are paid for their time.** All of it, including preparation and technology setup, and regardless of whether they finish the session.
3. **Every participant chooses how to take part.** Video, audio only, text chat or asynchronous. No mode is the "real" one.
4. **Private by default.** Participants always have a way to answer that the rest of the group cannot see.
5. **Consent is specific and ongoing.** Consent to take part, to be recorded, and to be quoted in a public task are three separate questions, and any of them can be withdrawn.
6. **Nothing leaves the room unchecked.** Before a finding becomes a public task, we check our interpretation back with the people who raised it. This mirrors the toolkit's Validation agent, which pauses at decisions that belong to the person.
7. **Findings become tasks, not notes.** A session is finished when its findings exist as clear tasks on the toolkit's to-do list, not when the recording ends.

## Field notes: where these practices come from

This kit draws on my research for Catholic Relief Services' ReMiND prenatal care app in Uttar Pradesh, India, where I led recruiting, consent, research, design and validation with low-vision and low-literacy residents alongside community health workers.

Three lessons from that work shape this kit:

- **Consent has to work for the person and their situation.** Prenatal care is culturally sensitive, and some women's fathers or husbands did not approve of their participation. We used written, oral and facilitator-supported consent together to keep participants safe. In this kit, consent is layered the same way (see `01-plan-and-recruit.md`).
- **People need a private way to answer.** We created a visual language of shapes and colors so participants could respond privately, in an encoded way, without needing to read. This kit carries that idea into remote sessions as "private response options" (see `02-discussion-guide.md`).
- **The interface should fit how people take in information.** The ReMiND app used audio and visual tools rather than relying on text. That is the same idea behind the toolkit's Adapter agent, and it is what these sessions are designed to inform.
