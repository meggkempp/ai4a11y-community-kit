# Step 1: Plan and recruit

Complete this page before inviting anyone. Each section ends with a short checklist.

## 1. Decide what the session is for

Write one sentence naming the decision the session should inform. For example:

> "Which adaptations should the next version of the Adapter agent prioritize for people who use screen magnification?"

A session without a decision attached produces interesting notes and no tasks.

- [ ] One-sentence purpose written
- [ ] Building block(s) in focus named: Ability profile, Adapter, Validation, or the full loop
- [ ] Core Contributor has reviewed the purpose and confirmed what they could act on in the next month

## 2. Recruit

**Recruit through trusted organizations, not cold outreach.** Disability-led organizations, peer networks, assistive technology user groups, and the Collective's existing community partners are the first route. Ask partners how their members prefer to be approached, and pay partner organizations for recruiting help when they ask for it.

**Recruit for range, not representativeness.** Aim for variety in how people interact with technology (screen readers, magnification, switch access, voice control, captions, plain language, sign language, cognitive supports), and include older adults and people who do not identify as disabled but whose needs change, since the toolkit is built for them too.

**Screen by what people use and ask for, never by diagnosis.** Example screener questions:

- Which of these do you use or change on your devices? (larger text, screen reader, voice control, captions, high contrast, keyboard only, reading aids, other)
- How would you like to take part? (video, audio only, text chat, in your own time)
- What would help you take part comfortably? (open answer)

- [ ] Recruiting partners contacted and asked how to approach members
- [ ] Screener uses needs and tools, not conditions
- [ ] Target mix of participants written down

## 3. Consent, layered

Consent is offered in more than one form, so it works for each person and their circumstances:

1. **Written, plain language.** A short consent page in an accessible format (screen-reader-friendly, large print, easy read on request).
2. **Spoken.** The facilitator reads the key points aloud at the start of the session and records verbal agreement.
3. **Supported.** Participants may bring a support person or ask a trusted facilitator to walk them through it, and may consent privately, without others present.

Ask three separate questions, each answerable on its own:

| Question | Default if unanswered |
|---|---|
| Do you agree to take part? | No |
| May we record this session? If yes, audio, video, or text only? | No recording |
| May we quote or paraphrase you, without your name, in a public task on the toolkit's GitHub? | Paraphrase only, no quotes |

The third question matters for open source: the toolkit's to-do list is public, so anything that becomes a task can be seen by anyone. Participants should know this before they speak, not after.

Participants can withdraw any consent at any time, including after the session, and their contributions will be removed from notes and tasks.

- [ ] Consent page written in plain language and available in requested formats
- [ ] Script for reading consent aloud prepared
- [ ] Three consent questions recorded per participant
- [ ] Process for withdrawal written down, including who to contact

## 4. Make the session accessible

Ask every participant in advance what they need, and confirm it before the day. Common accommodations:

- **Modes:** video with optional camera, audio only, live text chat, or asynchronous participation over a few days
- **Communication:** live captions (human captioning for sessions where accuracy matters), sign language interpreters booked early, plain-language versions of materials
- **Materials:** sent at least 48 hours ahead, in accessible formats; no information that exists only in an image or only spoken
- **Pacing:** sessions of 60 to 75 minutes at most, with a break; questions shared in advance; silence is fine
- **Technology:** participants use their own devices and assistive technology; offer a short paid tech check beforehand
- **Private response options:** at least one way to answer that the group cannot see (see the discussion guide)
- **Demos:** describe what is happening on screen out loud, and test that captions keep pace with the demo beforehand; send a written walkthrough of the demo for anyone following by text only

- [ ] Access needs collected from every participant
- [ ] Captioning and interpreters booked
- [ ] Materials sent in accessible formats 48 hours ahead
- [ ] Optional tech check offered

## 5. Pay people properly

- Pay for the full time, including preparation, the tech check and any asynchronous follow-up
- Pay in full even if someone leaves early or cannot finish
- Offer a choice of payment method (bank transfer, digital payment, gift card)
- Be aware that some people receiving disability benefits have income limits; offer payment options and let participants decide what works for them
- Pay promptly, within an agreed number of days, and tell participants the date

- [ ] Rate agreed with the Collective
- [ ] Payment methods offered
- [ ] Payment date communicated to participants

## 6. Safety and privacy

- Store recordings and notes in one access-controlled place; delete raw recordings on a set date
- Remove names and identifying details from anything shared beyond the research team
- Agree in advance what the facilitator does if a participant becomes distressed or discloses something sensitive
- Never ask participants to share their screen in a way that reveals personal information; offer to share a test page instead

- [ ] Storage location and deletion date set
- [ ] Distress and disclosure plan agreed
